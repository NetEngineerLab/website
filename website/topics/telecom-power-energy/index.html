Telecom Power & Energy

Plan site load, rectifier capacity, battery autonomy, generator fuel and solar support as connected parts of a telecom power system.

Start Here

Use the workflow below when planning or reviewing a telecom site power system. Begin with a measured DC load, then size conversion, storage and backup in sequence.

  1. Read the rectifier sizing guide to establish load, recharge and N-1 assumptions.
  2. Size rectifiers, then check battery autonomy and backup fuel.
  3. Validate equipment limits, battery age, generator operating rules and site measurements before approval.

Calculate and Plan

  • Telecom rectifier sizing — capacity, recharge and N-1 check.
  • -48 V battery runtime calculator — autonomy under the stated load.
  • Generator fuel consumption and runtime calculator — backup duration and fuel use.
  • Telecom solar and battery sizing — solar contribution and storage sizing.

Worked example: rectifier current then 4-hour battery

Cross-check with the telecom rectifier calculator and the -48 V battery runtime calculator. Rectifier numbers follow the published 180 A guide example.

1. Inputs and assumptions

InputValueAssumption
DC load180 A @ −48 VMeasured/defined site load boundary
Battery recharge60 ASteady recharge current after utility restore
Headroom / growth / recharge peak15% / 15% / 25%Planning factors from the rectifier guide
Battery autonomy target4 hoursAt the 180 A design load
DoD × ageing factor0.8 × 0.9Usable fraction of nameplate Ah

2. Formula and units

I combined (A) = (Iload × (1 + growth) + Irecharge × (1 + recharge peak)) × (1 + headroom)

Required Ah = (Iload × hours) ÷ (DoD × ageing)

Units: amperes (A), ampere-hours (Ah), hours (h).

3. Calculation

StepCalculationResult
Load with growth180 × 1.15207.0 A
Recharge with peak60 × 1.2575.0 A
Combined with headroom(207 + 75) × 1.15324.3 A
Battery Ah for 4 h(180 × 4) ÷ (0.8 × 0.9)1,000 Ah

4. Result and limits

Plan rectifier capacity around a 324.3 A combined scenario (then apply module size, derating, N+1 and N-1 checks in the calculator). For four hours at 180 A with the stated DoD/ageing factors, nameplate battery capacity is about 1,000 Ah.

Boundaries: does not replace vendor discharge curves, temperature derating, generator test data or maintenance rules. Age, temperature and faults can shorten runtime. Continue with rectifier sizing guide and generator fuel planning.

Common Engineering Scenarios

  • Rectifier capacity with recharge and N-1 — size operating and failed-unit states.
  • One-tank generator runtime — relate load, fuel curve and required autonomy.
  • One 100 Ah string at 600 W — Peukert runtime against a 4 hour target.
  • 60 m feeder, 16 mm2 or 25 mm2 — end voltage against a 46 V example floor.
  • Site expansion — recalculate DC load, rectifier reserve, battery runtime and generator margin together.

Failure, Resilience and Operations

Check the loss of one rectifier, reduced battery capacity, generator derating and solar shortfall. A nominal capacity result does not prove backup performance during a fault or an aged-battery condition.

Use the linked guides and calculator methodology with equipment data sheets, battery discharge curves and site operating rules. Record the load boundary, units, ambient conditions and design margin with each result.

Recommended Learning Path

  2. Size rectifiers and verify recharge/N-1.

Engineering Boundaries and FAQ

These browser tools do not replace manufacturer discharge curves, generator test data, temperature derating or an operational maintenance plan. Validate results against the installed equipment and site rules.

Why calculate rectifiers before batteries?

Rectifier reserve and recharge performance define the load that the battery system must support during normal and recovery states.

Does runtime equal guaranteed service time?

No. Runtime is an estimate from supplied load, battery and fuel assumptions; age, temperature, generator loading and faults can reduce it.

Task routes

Each telecom DC task has a place to start and a separate check. This hub owns the −48 V plant: rectifiers, batteries, generator fuel and site solar. Data-center UPS runtime, ride-through and AC UPS transfer stay on the data center power calculators hub and are not decided here.

  • Rectifier. Start in the rectifier sizing guide. Check with the rectifier calculator, including N-1.
  • Battery. Start in −48 V battery runtime. Check the worked example: 180 A for 4 h at 0.8 × 0.9 is 1,000 Ah nameplate.
  • Generator fuel. Start in the generator fuel guide. Check the fuel runtime calculator against the tank that can actually be drawn.
  • Solar. Start in solar and battery sizing. Check by repeating the run with the site’s low-sun hours. Do not borrow the data-center UPS to hide a shortfall.

Exception paths

  • Rectifier. Total amperes can pass while N-1 fails. Remove one module in the rectifier calculator before accepting the plant.
  • Battery. 1,000 Ah uses DoD 0.8 and ageing 0.9 only. If temperature or age is outside those factors, stop and use the manufacturer discharge curve.
  • Generator. A 30 kW set loaded at 18 kW for 12 h and 12 kW for 12 h, on the curve 1.5 / 2.5 / 4.2 / 5.8 / 7.4 L/h, burns 4.18 L/h. Twenty-four hours plus 10% reserve needs 111.5 L, and a 120 L tank runs about 25.8 h. If only 100 L is usable, runtime falls below 24 h: refuel or shed load. A load above the rated 30 kW is an overload, not a longer runtime.
  • Solar. 0.8 kW of telecom load for 24 h plus 0.1 kW auxiliary for 24 h is 21.6 kWh/day. At 3.5 peak sun hours, 80% system efficiency, 15% design margin, 550 W modules and 48 h backup (80% DoD, 92% battery efficiency, 10% battery margin, 48 V), required PV is 8.87 kW, which is 17 modules and 9.35 kWp, with about 1,345 Ah. If that 9.35 kWp array sees only 2.0 peak sun hours, generation is 14.96 kWh and the day is 6.64 kWh short.

Who owns the input

  • The radio or transport owner supplies the measured DC load in amperes or kilowatts. The power planner does not invent that load.
  • The power owner checks rectifier module size, derating, recharge and N-1.
  • The battery owner supplies the discharge curve when the 0.8 × 0.9 estimate is not valid.
  • The energy owner supplies the generator fuel curve, the usable tank volume and the solar-hour source.
  • Data-center UPS and ride-through inputs belong to the data-center power hub, not to this DC plant.

Field case: one outdoor site

Use one 180 A, −48 V site. Rectifier combined current at 15% growth, 60 A recharge, 25% recharge peak and 15% headroom is 324.3 A, then still run the module and N-1 check. Battery nameplate for 4 h is 1,000 Ah only under the stated factors. The 30 kW generator profile above needs 111.5 L for 24 h with reserve; do not book the whole 120 L tank as usable if the pickup sits higher. The solar array sized at 3.5 peak sun hours does not still cover 21.6 kWh when the same array is stuck at 2.0 peak sun hours.
