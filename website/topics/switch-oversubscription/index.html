Formula direction

Line-rate oversubscription = access line rate ÷ the line rate of uplinks that are forwarding, written as X:1. The denominator is the uplink. Full duplex does not add both directions into that number. The ratio is not congestion: offered load, direction, bursts, packet rate, and port or hardware limits are separate checks. Recompute 1:1, 2:1, 4:1 and 8:1, plus 10G, 25G, 40G and 100G examples, in the ratio guide. One lost link in an N+1 set is in the N-1 and N+1 guide. A hash that pins one flow to one member is in LACP member congestion.

Worked example: 48×1G access with 2×10G uplinks

Static engineering case you can re-enter in the switch uplink oversubscription calculator. Numbers match the N-1 guide examples on this topic.

1. Inputs and assumptions

InputValueAssumption
Access ports × speed48 × 1 GbpsAll ports in the demand pool
Uplinks2 × 10 Gbps (LACP)Members share load in the normal state
Busy-hour demand13 Gbps toward the uplinkPlanning estimate, not line-rate on every port
Failure caseOne 10G member down (N-1)Remaining capacity = 10 Gbps

2. Formula and units

Theoretical oversubscription = (access ports × access speed) ÷ (uplink count × uplink speed)

Normal headroom = uplink capacity − busy-hour demand

N-1 headroom = surviving uplink capacity − busy-hour demand

Units: Gbps throughout.

3. Calculation

StepCalculationResult
Access pool48 × 148 Gbps
Normal uplink capacity2 × 1020 Gbps
Theoretical ratio48 ÷ 202.4:1
Normal headroom20 − 13+7 Gbps
N-1 capacity1 × 1010 Gbps
N-1 headroom10 − 13−3 Gbps (deficit)

4. Result and limits

Normal state can carry the 13 Gbps busy-hour estimate; the single-uplink failure state cannot. A 2×10G design for this demand needs either lower busy-hour traffic, a third/fourth 10G member, or 25G-class uplinks before approval.

Boundaries: ignores LAG hash imbalance, microbursts, and oversubscription inside the fabric. Not a substitute for measured counters or vendor QoS limits. Cross-check with the N-1 guide and the calculator.

Task routes

Each switching task has a guide to start and a separate check. This hub owns access-switch uplink ratio, LAG and N-1. It does not grade the page as Authority; that label waits for an independent acceptance record.

  • Ratio. Start in the oversubscription formula guide. Check 48 × 1 Gbps ÷ 20 Gbps = 2.4:1.
  • 48-port options. Start in the 48-port examples. Check 2×10G, 4×10G and 2×25G against the same access pool.
  • Busy-hour demand. Start in is 2×10G enough. The published 13 Gbps demand leaves 20 − 13 = +7 Gbps in the normal state.
  • N-1. Start in the N-1 failure guide. One 10G member down leaves 10 Gbps, and 10 − 13 = −3 Gbps. Do not approve the design on the normal state alone.
  • Uneven LAG. Start in LACP member congestion. Aggregate headroom does not prove each member is clear.

Failure & Resilience

Oversubscription should not be approved using normal-state bandwidth alone. A resilient design should also be checked after the loss of an uplink or other required N-1 component.

Engineering Boundaries and FAQ

Results depend on busy-hour traffic, link direction, LAG hashing, failure topology and equipment limits. Validate with telemetry and a design review before procurement.

Does the ratio alone prove capacity?

No. Check measured utilization, traffic distribution and N-1 surviving capacity.
