After 90 m, Does 26 AWG Drop the Camera Below 42.5 V?

Example only, not a certified cable test. A 25.5 W load over 90 m ends at 37.87 V on 26 AWG and 43.51 V on 23 AWG. The watt budget can pass while this voltage check fails.

The previous camera example asks whether the switch watt budget is large enough. This one asks whether the voltage at the device is still above an entered floor.

Example assumptions

  • Length 90 m. Source 48 V. Load at the device 25.5 W. Temperature 45°C. Contact resistance 0.3 ohm. Delivery efficiency 90%. Minimum voltage 42.5 V. Two pairs. Class label 802.3at.
  • Compare 26 AWG with 23 AWG. The 42.5 V floor is an example input.

Steps

  1. 26 AWG: drop 10.13 V, remote voltage 37.87 V, loss 21.1%. The calculator status is fail because 37.87 V is below 42.5 V.
  2. 23 AWG: drop 4.49 V, remote voltage 43.51 V, loss 9.4%. Status is pass.

Boundary

Changing the wire gauge does not change the switch's total watt budget. The resistance table and the pair count belong to this calculator. IEEE 802.3 names the class; it is not a measured cable certificate. Type the real length, load and minimum voltage.

Public source: https://www.ieee802.org/3/.
