How Many Tons of Cooling Does a 5 kW Server Closet Need?

Example only, not a surveyed closet. Five kilowatts of IT plus the small room gains in this sheet become 7.268 kW of design sensible load, about 2.07 tons. One 8 kW sensible unit covers normal operation. N+1 needs a second unit that can also carry 7.268 kW alone.

The cooling topic already carries a 100 kW IT hall and the heat-load guide carries a 120 kW sheet. This example is a single closet, where a unit that looks larger than the IT nameplate can still be the wrong N+1 choice.

Example assumptions

  • IT 5 kW, UPS loss 0.4 kW, PDU loss 0.2 kW, lighting 0.2 kW, one person at 120 W, envelope 0.3 kW, other sensible heat 0.1 kW.
  • Headroom 15%. Unit sensible capacity 8 kW. Supply-to-return difference 10°C. No extra latent load.

Steps

  1. People = 0.12 kW. Base sensible load = 6.32 kW.
  2. Design load = 6.32 × 1.15 = 7.268 kW.
  3. Tons = 7.268 / 3.5168525 = 2.07 tons. That is sensible refrigeration tons, not a comfort-cooling nameplate.
  4. One 8 kW unit: normal capacity 8 kW, which covers 7.268 kW. After it stops, remaining capacity is 0 kW, so N+1 fails.
  5. Two 8 kW units: normal capacity 16 kW. After one stops, 8 kW still covers 7.268 kW, so this N+1 check passes. The engine’s active count is 1 and the recommended count is 2.

Boundary

Do not size the closet as “5 kW is under 2 tons, so a 2-ton comfort unit is enough.” Comfort total capacity is not sensible capacity at server-room conditions. The calculator does not model airflow short-circuit or a unit that cannot hold its 8 kW sensible rating at the room temperature. This is not the 100 kW hall case.

Sources: Schneider Electric, Calculating Total Cooling Requirements for Data Centers; ASHRAE Datacom Series.
