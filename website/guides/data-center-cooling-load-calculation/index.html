DATA CENTER COOLING GUIDE

Data Center Cooling Load Formula & Sizing Guide

A practical method for converting room heat sources into design cooling capacity, unit count and N+1 verification.

Sensible LoadPrecision CoolingN+1Cooling Tons

Quick answer

Add every sensible heat source released inside the controlled room, then apply an explicit design margin. Divide the resulting design load by the sensible capacity of one precision cooling unit, round up, and add the required standby units.

Core formula

Base sensible load = IT load + UPS loss + PDU loss + lighting + people + envelope gain + other sensible load.

Design load = Base sensible load × (1 + engineering margin).

Which heat sources belong in the calculation?

IT electrical demand normally becomes heat in the room. Add UPS and PDU conversion losses only when that heat is released in the same conditioned space. Include lighting, occupants, solar or envelope gains and any other equipment. Do not count a loss twice when it is already outside the cooling boundary.

Worked example: 120 kW IT room

InputValue
IT equipment120 kW
UPS loss6 kW
PDU loss3 kW
Lighting2 kW
4 people at 120 W0.48 kW
Envelope gain5 kW
Other sensible heat2 kW

The base sensible load is 138.48 kW. With a 15% margin, the design load is 159.25 kW, equivalent to about 45.3 refrigeration tons. With 50 kW sensible-capacity units, four units must run; an N+1 design therefore recommends five units. After one unit is unavailable, 200 kW remains, so this example passes the N+1 capacity check.

Use sensible capacity, not the nameplate total

Server rooms are usually dominated by sensible heat. Select against manufacturer sensible capacity at the expected return-air temperature, humidity, outdoor condition, altitude and airflow. A nominal total-cooling rating can overstate the useful capacity for an IT room.

Airflow estimate

For air cooling, a first-pass airflow estimate is Q × 3600 ÷ (ρ × cp × ΔT). At 159.25 kW, 1.2 kg/m³ air density, 1.006 kJ/(kg·K) and a 10°C temperature rise, airflow is about 47,490 m³/h. Treat this as a room-level balance; rack inlet conditions and bypass or recirculation still need validation.

Engineering workflow

  1. Define the cooling boundary.
  2. Measure or forecast IT demand.
  3. Add only heat released inside that boundary.
  4. Apply a documented margin.
  5. Size with actual sensible capacity.
  6. Verify N+1 and airflow distribution.

Frequently asked questions

Is IT power equal to cooling load?

It is a strong first-order estimate because nearly all IT electrical energy becomes heat, but room losses and environmental gains must still be added.

Should the design use peak or average IT load?

Use a justified design scenario that captures credible peak demand and planned growth. An annual average is not a safe capacity basis.

Does N+1 mean adding one unit?

Yes, after finding the number of units required to carry the design load, add one standby unit and verify that the remaining units still carry the load.
