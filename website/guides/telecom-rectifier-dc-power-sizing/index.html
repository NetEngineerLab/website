Telecom Rectifier Sizing: N+1/N+2, Recharge and Failure Check

A resilient -48 V DC plant must survive growth, battery recharge peaks and module failures while staying inside shelf and utilization limits.

-48 V DCRectifier sizingN+1/N+2N-1/N-2

Quick answer

Include live load and recharge current, apply engineering headroom, round up active modules, then add the selected redundancy. Check the same design current after one and two modules are unavailable.

Sizing formula

I required = (I load + I recharge) × (1 + headroom)

I planning,module = I module × derating × target utilization

N recommended = ceil(I required ÷ I planning,module) + N redundancy

N-1 capacity = (N installed − 1) × I module × derating

180 A load example

For 180 A load, 60 A recharge, 15% headroom, 15% growth and a 25% recharge peak, the combined scenario is (180 × 1.15 + 60 × 1.25) × 1.15 = 324.3 A. A 50 A module and N+1 policy must then be checked against target utilization and derating; the calculator recommends seven modules for the current 180 A + 60 A baseline.

Design checks that change the answer

  • Apply temperature and altitude derating before failure capacity checks.
  • Respect shelf maximum modules and available slots; report expansion modules or recharge limiting when the frame is full.
  • Compare normal utilization with N-1 and N-2 surviving capacity.
  • Use estimated AC input as a planning value and verify protection, conductors, grounding and vendor limits.

Frequently asked questions

Why include battery recharge current?

The rectifier must carry the live telecom load and recharge the battery after utility restoration, which can create the highest steady demand.

How are N+1 and N+2 modules sized?

Round up the active modules required for load, recharge and headroom, then add one or two redundant modules.

What does N-1 failure capacity prove?

It checks whether the remaining installed modules still carry the design current after one module is unavailable.
