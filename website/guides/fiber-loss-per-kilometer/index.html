Fiber Loss per Kilometer: dB/km Formula, Calculation and Engineering Examples

A dB/km coefficient times length is the fiber term, in dB. Connectors, splices and splitters are extra. The fiber term is not the total link loss, and a loss in dB is not a power level in dBm.

dB and dBm

Loss in dB is a ratio: transmit power in dBm minus receive power in dBm. Zero dBm is 1 mW. A ratio of two in power is 10 log10(2) = 3.010 dB. That 3.010 dB can sit on a 0 dBm launch or a −20 dBm launch. The ratio does not name the power.

Attenuation per kilometre

For one coefficient along the fiber, Afiber = α × L. α is in dB/km and L is in km, so Afiber is in dB. This is only the fiber term.

The in-force text is ITU-T G.652 (08/2024), approved 29 August 2024. G.652 (11/2016) is superseded. Clause 8, Table 2 is only the G.652.D attribute table. Its cable attenuation maxima are 0.40 dB/km from 1310 nm to 1625 nm and 0.30 dB/km across 1530–1565 nm, so 1550 nm is inside that band rather than a separate cell. Note 1 excludes short jumper, indoor and drop cables. These cells are not the G.652.B cable row, which lists 0.4, 0.35 and 0.4 dB/km at 1310, 1550 and 1625 nm. They are category maxima, not a measured span and not a typical cable. Public text: ITU-T G.652 (08/2024). Series index: ITU-T G.652. ITU-T G.650.1 is the recommendation for test methods of linear attributes, including attenuation. This page does not copy a test procedure from it. Public text: ITU-T G.650.1.

This site’s fiber loss calculator still offers planning defaults of 0.35 dB/km at 1310 nm and 0.25 dB/km at 1550 nm. Both sit below the G.652.D maxima. They are planning inputs already used on that calculator, not measurements and not the standard maximum.

A G.657 cable is not identified by copying the G.652.D maximum. Bending loss is outside this coefficient. If the cable category is not known, the coefficient is UNKNOWN. The 300 m OM4 example elsewhere on this site uses 3.0 dB/km as that example’s assumption. This page does not treat 3.0 dB/km as an OM4 maximum. The cabled multimode maximum is UNKNOWN until the cable specification is read.

Worked example: 12 km at the 1550 nm maximum

Category G.652.D, 1550 nm, α = 0.30 dB/km, L = 12 km. Fiber term = 0.30 × 12 = 3.60 dB. The same 12 km at the 1310 nm maximum of 0.40 dB/km is 0.40 × 12 = 4.80 dB. The length did not change. The wavelength did.

The 3.60 dB fiber term is not the link. Add two connector pairs at 0.50 dB each, which is 1.00 dB, and three fusion splices at 0.10 dB each, which is 0.30 dB. Those connector and splice values are assumptions for this example, not a connector standard and not a measured OTDR event. Physical path loss = 3.60 + 1.00 + 0.30 = 4.90 dB.

Dividing that path by length gives 4.90 / 12 = 0.408 dB/km. That blended figure is not the fiber coefficient. It has folded the connectors and splices into a per-kilometre number.

With an assumed transmit power of 0 dBm, estimated receive power is 0 − 4.90 = −4.90 dBm. The 4.90 dB is the loss ratio. The −4.90 dBm is the power level. This transmit power is an assumption, not an optic class. Engineering margin is not part of this 4.90 dB.

The total link budget, including margin and the transceiver window, stays on the fiber link budget guide. Its 10 km case uses 0.35 dB/km and a 3 dB margin and is a different calculation. Splitter loss stays on the PON splitter guide. Compare the finished path with the transmitter and receiver window in the optical power budget calculator.

Measurement mistakes

  • Reading end-to-end loss divided by length as if it were α.
  • Using a 1550 nm coefficient on a 1310 nm or 1490 nm path.
  • Calling a dirty connector or a tight bend fiber attenuation.
  • Treating the G.652.D maximum as the loss a span will measure.
  • Treating the calculator’s 0.25 dB/km default as a cable specification.

A fiber term inside the window does not by itself show that the receiver is inside its datasheet window. That check belongs to the power budget.
