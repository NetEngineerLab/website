What Does a 1.50 PUE From One Month of Meters Actually Say?

Example only, not a metered year and not a redundancy test. Facility energy of 120000 kWh and IT energy of 80000 kWh in 30 days give PUE 1.50. Against a 1.40 target the calculator marks caution.

Cooling tons and UPS minutes answer failure cases. PUE answers an energy ratio for one period.

Example assumptions

  • Energy mode. Same 30 days. Facility total 120000 kWh. IT 80000 kWh. Target PUE 1.40.
  • The non-IT split in this sheet is cooling 25000, UPS 8000, lighting 2000, distribution 3000 and other 2000 kWh. Those parts sum to the 40000 kWh overhead.

Steps

  1. PUE = 120000 / 80000 = 1.50. Non-IT energy = 40000 kWh. IT is 66.67% of the facility total.
  2. 1.50 is above 1.40 and within 0.20 of the target, so the calculator status is caution.
  3. The engine annualizes a 30-day total by 365/30. That product is 1460000 kWh. It is not a measured year.
  4. If IT later grows 20% and the non-IT energy stays fixed, the same engine reports a fixed-overhead PUE of 1.417. That is the calculator's default growth illustration, not a forecast.

Boundary

PUE does not say a cooling unit or a UPS module can fail and still carry the load. ISO/IEC 30134-2 is the public catalog entry for the PUE data-centre metric; this page does not quote a clause and does not claim a certified PUE.

Public source: https://www.iso.org/standard/30134-2.
