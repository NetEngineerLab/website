PUE definition

The PUE definition used for this calculation is a ratio. The numerator is the data-center energy inside a stated boundary. The denominator is the IT equipment energy inside that same boundary. Both terms use the same period. The public catalog for the current standard, ISO/IEC 30134-2:2026, says the standard defines how to measure, calculate, report and interpret power usage effectiveness, and that it introduces measurement categories. It also says the 2026 edition adds guidance for mixed-use buildings, unaccounted energy and on-site generation. This page does not quote those paywalled clauses. The 2016 edition is withdrawn.

The Green Grid WP#49, hosted by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, states the public ratio in the same way: total facility energy divided by IT equipment energy. Total facility energy is the energy dedicated to the data center, at the utility meter of a dedicated facility or at the data-center portion of a mixed-use building. IT equipment energy is the energy used to manage, process, store or route data in the compute space, including the IT equipment and the supplemental equipment used to monitor or control it. Power delivery, cooling-system electricity and data-center lighting sit in the total and outside the IT term.

PUE calculation formula

PUE = Etotal ÷ EIT

When the cooling electricity and the other supporting loads are inside the facility total and outside the IT meter, the same identity is:

PUE = 1 + Ecooling ÷ EIT + Eother ÷ EIT

The ratio has no unit when both energies use the same unit. A lower ratio means less supporting energy per unit of IT energy. It does not, by itself, say that facility energy went down.

kW and kWh are different quantities

A kilowatt is power, the rate at one moment. A kilowatt-hour is energy, that rate integrated over time. WP#49 section 4.10 says PUE can be computed from either, and that an energy measurement accumulates the flow while a power measurement is only a snapshot. The same section says public reporting treats PUE as an energy metric. If a load of 400 kW stayed constant for one hour, the energy would be 400 kWh. That sentence is the unit relationship. It is not a reading from a site.

An instantaneous ratio in kW is not the period ratio in kWh unless the power was constant for the whole period. This page keeps the two sheets separate.

Calculation boundary

Write the boundary before dividing. The facility total includes every energy flow the chosen boundary assigns to the data center, and excludes energy that serves something else on a shared meter. The IT term uses one placement for the whole period. WP#49 describes three public placements for that IT meter: the UPS output, the PDU output, and the IT-equipment input. Moving the meter changes the denominator. ISO/IEC 30134-2:2026 introduces measurement categories in the standard. This page does not treat the 2012 placement names as the 2026 clause text.

Compare two results only when the period, the facility boundary and the IT placement match. Leaving cooling electricity out of the numerator lowers the ratio without a change in cooling performance. That is a boundary change. The catalog names an mPUE variant for mixed-use buildings. The sheet below assumes the facility total is already the data-center portion only. It does not calculate mPUE, and it does not assign a credit for on-site generation.

Worked example, assumed and not measured

These inputs are chosen so the arithmetic can be checked against the calculator. They are not meter readings, not a commissioning record, and not a published average. The IT placement assumed for the sheet is the PDU output. The period is 30 days. Every row is inside that one boundary.

Assumed inputEnergy
IT equipment energy100000 kWh
Cooling electrical energy32000 kWh
UPS and electrical loss outside the IT meter10000 kWh
Lighting3000 kWh
Remaining distribution energy3000 kWh
Other supporting energy2000 kWh

The supporting rows sum to 50000 kWh. Facility total = 100000 + 50000 = 150000 kWh. PUE = 150000 ÷ 100000 = 1.50. Cooling ÷ IT = 32000 ÷ 100000 = 0.32. The other supporting energy is 18000 kWh, so 18000 ÷ 100000 = 0.18. Then 1 + 0.32 + 0.18 = 1.50. IT is 100000 ÷ 150000 of the facility total, which is 66.67% when rounded to two decimal places. Cooling is 32000 ÷ 50000 = 0.64 of the supporting energy in this sheet.

A second sheet is a different assumption, not a restatement of the month. At one moment the facility power is 560 kW and the IT power is 400 kW, with supporting power of 90 kW cooling, 40 kW UPS, 10 kW lighting, 12 kW distribution and 8 kW other. Those supporting powers sum to 160 kW, and 400 + 160 = 560. The snapshot ratio is 560 ÷ 400 = 1.40. That 1.40 is not the 30-day PUE of 1.50. Multiplying 400 kW by 720 hours gives 288000 kWh only if the power never changes. This example does not assume that.

In energy mode the calculator scales a period total by 365 ÷ period days when it shows an annual figure. For this assumed 30-day total, 150000 × 365 ÷ 30 = 1825000 kWh. That product is the scaling, not a measured year. If a target PUE is typed in, the calculator compares IT energy times that target with the scaled total. The difference is a planning illustration for the typed target. This page does not recommend a target and does not state a savings percentage.

PUE and cooling energy

Cooling capacity, in kilowatts of heat removed or in refrigeration tons, answers whether the plant can remove the room heat. That question belongs to the Data Center Cooling Load Formula & Sizing Guide, the Server Room Heat Load Calculation: What to Include page, and the Data Center Cooling Load & Precision AC Capacity Calculator. The Data Center Cooling Load & Precision Cooling Capacity Hub keeps those capacity checks together.

PUE uses the electrical energy the cooling system draws during the same period, and only when that electricity is inside the facility boundary and outside the IT boundary. Nameplate tons do not appear in the formula. In the assumed month, the 32000 kWh cooling row is what moves the ratio, not a tonnage rating.

The cooling calculator can correct an airflow estimate for local dry-air density. That correction changes the volume flow for a stated temperature rise. It does not derate cooling capacity, and it is not an input to this PUE calculation.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a kW snapshot as the period PUE.
  • Comparing two ratios whose periods, facility boundaries or IT meter placements differ.
  • Dropping the cooling meter from the numerator and calling the lower ratio an efficiency gain.
  • Treating a lower ratio that comes only from higher IT energy, with supporting energy unchanged, as a reduction in facility energy.
  • Using PUE to decide whether one cooling unit or one UPS module can fail. That is a different question, worked in What Does a 1.50 PUE From One Month of Meters Actually Say?
  • Publishing a target-PUE difference as a measured saving.

What to check next

Keep one written boundary and one period before comparing. Say whether cooling electricity is inside that boundary. Use kWh for the period result, and label any kW reading as a snapshot. Read the facility kilowatt-hours as well as the ratio. Size the cooling plant on the cooling calculator. This page does not state a mean value for PUE and does not state a savings percentage.
