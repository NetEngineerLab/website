Fiber Link Budget Calculation: Loss, Margin and Maximum Distance

Plan a fiber link by adding connector, splice and fiber attenuation losses, then reserve engineering margin before comparing the result with transceiver power limits.

Open the fiber link budget calculator

A fiber link works when the transmitter-to-receiver optical power window is wider than total path loss. A link budget makes every loss assumption visible.

Total loss = attenuation * distance + connector loss + splice loss + other passive loss
Margin = available budget - total loss

The attenuation term is the coefficient in dB/km times distance. The 0.35 dB/km and 0.22 dB/km figures on this page are assumptions for these cases, not a G.652.D maximum and not a measurement. The coefficient itself, including a 12 km case that is 3.60 dB of fiber before connectors, is in fiber loss per kilometre.

Worked example: 10 km single-mode link

Assume 0.35 dB/km attenuation, two connectors at 0.5 dB each, four splices at 0.1 dB each and 3 dB engineering margin. Design loss is 7.9 dB.

Two reach cases

Both cases use the fiber loss calculator. Transmit power and receive threshold are planning assumptions for the optic class, not a vendor certification.

  • 10 km, 1310 nm class. 0.35 dB/km, two connector pairs at 0.5 dB, four splices at 0.1 dB and 3 dB margin. Design loss is 7.9 dB. With transmit 0 dBm and receive threshold −14 dBm, the 14 dB budget leaves 6.1 dB. At the same loss rates the fiber term can grow until the budget is used, which is 27.4 km.
  • 40 km, 1550 nm class. 0.22 dB/km, two connector pairs at 0.3 dB, eight splices at 0.05 dB and 3 dB margin. Design loss is 12.8 dB. With transmit +2 dBm and receive threshold −28 dBm, the 30 dB budget leaves 17.2 dB. The same 12.8 dB path against a 12 dB short-reach budget fails. Do not stretch a 10 km optic to 40 km because the loss formula still returns a number.

Approval workflow

  1. Record transceiver transmit and receive limits.
  2. Measure or estimate every passive loss.
  3. Add margin before comparing to the power budget.
  4. Check both directions for asymmetric optics.

Frequently asked questions

What does a negative margin mean?

The path loss exceeds the optical budget. Reduce loss or select optics with a larger budget.

Should connector loss be counted once or twice?

Count each mated connector pair in the optical path.
