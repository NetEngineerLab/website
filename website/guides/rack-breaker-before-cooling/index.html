At 4 kW, Does the 16 A Breaker Fail Before the Cooling?

Example only, not a rack audit. Ten 500 W devices at 80% utilization are 4000 W. On a 230 V single-phase 16 A breaker the current is 18.31 A, 114.4% of the breaker. The cooling load for that heat is 1432.15 W, about 1.222 tons.

The cooling topic already converts room heat to tons. This example asks which limit binds inside one cabinet: the entered breaker or the cooling watts.

Example assumptions

  • Ten devices at 500 W nameplate. Utilization 80%. No growth and no extra redundancy. Power factor 0.95. 230 V, one phase, 16 A breaker.
  • UPS efficiency 0.95. PDU efficiency 0.98. No latent share. Cooling COP 3. One feed, not dual path.

Steps

  1. Actual power = 10 × 500 × 0.80 = 4000 W. Apparent power = 4000 / 0.95 = 4210.53 VA.
  2. Current = 4210.53 / 230 = 18.31 A. That is 114.4% of 16 A, so the calculator marks the electrical check as fail.
  3. Heat at the rack = 4000 / (0.95 × 0.98) = 4296.46 W. Cooling input = 4296.46 / 3 = 1432.15 W, or 1.222 tons.

Boundary

The 80% warning line belongs to this calculator. This example fails because 18.31 A is already above the entered 16 A breaker, not because a code article was applied. The ton figure is not a room N+1 design. ASHRAE Datacom is the thermal-context reference; it does not certify this cabinet.

Public source: https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/bookstore/datacom-series.
