How Many 10G Uplinks Does a 48-Port Switch Need at 40% Utilization?

Example only. The familiar 2.4:1 ratio stays 2.4:1 with two 10G members, but 40% busy-hour utilization produces 19.2 Gbps. That fits inside 20 Gbps and still misses an 80% safe target. Three 10G members pass.

Existing 48-port pages answer whether 2×10G is enough for lighter busy-hour estimates such as 10, 12 or 13 Gbps. This example asks a different question: how many 10G members are required when the planning utilization is 40% and the safe target is 80% of line rate.

Example assumptions

  • 48 × 1 Gbps, utilization 40%, concurrency 100%, overhead 0%.
  • Target 80%. Each 10G member therefore contributes 8 Gbps of safe capacity.

Steps

  1. Demand = 48 × 0.40 = 19.2 Gbps.
  2. Two members: capacity 20 Gbps, safe capacity 16 Gbps, spare 16 − 19.2 = -3.2 Gbps, demand utilization 96%, ratio still 2.4:1. Status warning. Required member count at the safe target is 3.
  3. Three members: capacity 30 Gbps, safe capacity 24 Gbps, spare 4.8 Gbps, ratio 48 / 30 = 1.6:1. Status pass.

Boundary

Passing with three members is a normal-state check. It does not prove that two surviving members can carry 19.2 Gbps inside the 80% target after one failure. Rerun with one member removed before calling the bundle N-1. The 40% input is an example, not a recommended campus standard.

Source: Cisco campus oversubscription note. Cisco’s historical 20:1 access rule of thumb is not used as a pass criterion here.
