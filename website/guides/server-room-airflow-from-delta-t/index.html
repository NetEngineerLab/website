How Much Airflow Does a Server Room Need at a 6°C Delta-T?

Example only. A room with 18 kW of IT and the gains below designs to 24.909 kW, about 7.08 tons, at either temperature rise. Changing the supply-to-return difference from 10°C to 6°C raises the required airflow from about 7428 m³/h to about 12380 m³/h.

Tons answer how much heat must be removed. Airflow answers whether the selected units can move that heat across the temperature rise you actually operate. This example holds the heat sheet still and changes only delta-T.

Example assumptions

  • IT 18 kW, UPS loss 1.44 kW, lighting 0.48 kW, two people at 120 W, other sensible heat 1.5 kW, envelope 0.
  • Headroom 15%. Two installed units at 15 kW sensible each. Compare delta-T of 10°C and 6°C.

Steps

  1. Base load = 21.66 kW. Design load = 21.66 × 1.15 = 24.909 kW. Tons = 7.08. Both delta-T runs use this same heat.
  2. The calculator estimates airflow as design kW × 3600 / (1.2 × 1.006 × delta-T). At 10°C that is about 7428 m³/h. At 6°C it is about 12380 m³/h.
  3. Two 15 kW units provide 30 kW normally and 15 kW after one stop. 15 is below 24.909, so N+1 fails at either delta-T. Airflow did not repair the missing unit.

Boundary

The airflow figure is a sensible-heat estimate at the stated density and specific heat. It is not a measurement of tile flow, bypass or containment. A lower delta-T needs more air for the same heat; it does not reduce the tons. Selecting catalog tons without checking fan capacity at 6°C leaves the room short of air.

Sources: ASHRAE Datacom Series; Schneider Electric cooling-requirements white paper.
