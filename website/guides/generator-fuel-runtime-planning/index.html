How long will a telecom generator run on one tank?

For a telecom site, tank size alone does not determine outage autonomy. Estimate runtime from usable fuel and the generator’s fuel rate across the site’s changing load profile. Use the exact model’s manufacturer curve when available; the example below shows the calculation, not a universal diesel-consumption standard.

What problem are you solving?

During a utility outage, the operations team needs to know whether on-site fuel covers the expected outage, when a refill is required, and how much fuel to stage. A nameplate tank volume divided by a single “litres per hour” figure can mislead when the site load changes between daytime radio, cooling and overnight conditions.

Why load and usable fuel matter

Fuel consumption is model- and operating-point-specific. A generator’s fuel curve reflects its engine, alternator, calibration and emissions package. Also, nominal tank volume may include fuel that operations intentionally reserves or cannot draw because of pickup geometry and site policy.

Calculation method

For each time segment, convert site load to generator load percentage, then interpolate the manufacturer’s fuel curve between adjacent points. Multiply that segment’s fuel rate by its duration and add the segments.

Fuel used = Σ (interpolated fuel rateᵢ × hoursᵢ)

Usable fuel = tank volume × (1 − reserve fraction) − unusable fuel

Runtime ≈ usable fuel ÷ profile-weighted average fuel rate

The weighted average is total fuel over the profile divided by total profile hours. For an outage target longer than the entered profile, repeating that profile assumes the same load cycle continues.

Worked example

Assumptions (illustrative only): 30 kW generator; manufacturer-like example curve entered as 0% = 1.5, 25% = 2.5, 50% = 4.2, 75% = 5.8 and 100% = 7.4 L/h; 120 L nominal tank; 10% reserve; zero separately entered unusable fuel. These are example inputs, not a vendor specification or industry standard.

Assume 18 kW for 12 hours, followed by 12 kW for 12 hours. Linear interpolation gives 4.84 L/h at 60% load and 3.52 L/h at 40% load. Daily fuel use is (4.84 × 12) + (3.52 × 12) = 100.32 L per 24 hours. Usable fuel is 120 × (1 − 0.10) = 108 L. At this repeated profile, estimated runtime is 108 ÷ (100.32 ÷ 24) = about 25.8 hours.

This leaves little margin beyond a one-day outage. Real consumption, tank drawdown, refuelling access and changing site loads can reduce that margin; do not treat 25.8 hours as a guaranteed runtime.

Common mistakes

  • Using generator rated kW as if it were the site’s continuous load.
  • Dividing nominal tank volume by a full-load or no-load fuel figure.
  • Counting reserve fuel as available, or forgetting explicitly unusable tank volume.
  • Assuming the daytime load persists overnight, or that a short profile repeats unchanged for a multi-day outage.
  • Treating battery bridge energy as fuel-saving runtime without checking the battery’s discharge curve and transfer sequence.

How to improve the estimate

Obtain the fuel-consumption table for the exact generator model and enter the site’s measured or defensible time-segment loads. Confirm ambient-temperature and altitude derating, minimum permitted loading, transient starting loads, tank usable volume, fuel-return arrangement and refill logistics. For longer outages, model delivery intervals and verify the planned operating sequence with the generator supplier and site procedures.

FAQ

How do I calculate generator runtime from tank size?

Use usable fuel divided by the average fuel rate for the actual load profile. The rate should come from the exact generator model fuel curve.

Can I use a generic litres-per-hour value?

Only for a rough screening estimate. Engine, alternator, emissions package and load point change consumption; use the manufacturer's data for design.

Does battery autonomy count as generator runtime?

No. Battery autonomy can delay generator start or bridge transfer, but it is a separate energy reserve and must be checked against the battery discharge characteristics.
