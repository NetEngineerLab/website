How Many Connector Pairs Can a 2 km Campus Fiber Link Carry?

Example only. Two kilometres at 0.35 dB/km is only 0.7 dB of fiber. Six mated pairs at 0.75 dB, two fusion splices and 3 dB of margin make the design loss 8.4 dB. A 14 dB class budget still passes. An 8 dB short-reach budget fails by 0.4 dB.

Campus fiber often fails in the patch fields at the two buildings and at intermediate panels, not in the cable attenuation. This example counts those pairs. It is not the 10 km or 40 km reach case.

Example assumptions

  • Distance 2 km, attenuation 0.35 dB/km, two fusion splices at 0.10 dB, six mated pairs at 0.75 dB, engineering margin 3 dB. No splitter.
  • Longer class: transmit 0 dBm, receive threshold -14 dBm, available budget 14 dB.
  • Short-reach class on the same plant: transmit -3 dBm, receive threshold -11 dBm, available budget 8 dB.

Steps

  1. Fiber = 0.70 dB. Splices = 0.20 dB. Connectors = 4.50 dB. Physical loss = 5.40 dB. Design loss = 8.40 dB.
  2. Against 14 dB, remaining budget = 5.60 dB. Estimated receive power = -5.4 dBm. Receive margin = 8.6 dB. Status healthy.
  3. Against 8 dB, remaining budget = -0.4 dB. Estimated receive power = -8.4 dBm. Receive margin = 2.6 dB. Status failed, because the design loss exceeds the short-reach budget.

Boundary

Six pairs is an example count for two building entrances plus one intermediate panel, with a pair at each appearance. A real OTDR or loss-test set replaces the 0.75 dB screen. Do not stretch an 8 dB optic across this plant because the fiber term alone is only 0.7 dB. Splitter cascades belong on the PON splitter guide, not in this path.

Sources: ITU-T G.652; The Fiber Optic Association, loss budget.
