About and applicability

The Mathis ceiling is an upper-bound model for loss-based TCP congestion avoidance, not a throughput test. The optional bandwidth-delay product shows how many bytes fill the pipe at the link rate you enter.

This is an engineering approximation for planning. It is not a laboratory measurement and it does not guarantee equipment performance.

Content reviewed: 2026-11-19

How it is calculated

Rate < (MSS × 8) / (RTT × √p). BDP bytes = link bit rate × RTT / 8.

Mathis, Semke, Mahdavi and Ott described the macroscopic rate of TCP congestion avoidance as about one MSS per RTT times 1/√p, where p is the loss probability. This page uses Rate = (MSS bytes × 8) / (RTT seconds × √p) and labels it a ceiling. A worked case is MSS 1460 bytes, RTT 40 ms and p = 0.0001: √p = 0.01 and the rate is 29.2 Mbps. BDP follows RFC 6349's bandwidth-delay product once you supply a link rate. Delayed ACK, cubic, pacing and application limits are not in the formula.

FAQ

Does a lower loss always raise the result?

In this model, yes, because the ceiling divides by √p.

Is the BDP required?

No. Leave the link rate blank and the BDP stays blank.

Why is 1 percent loss entered as 0.01 with the percent unit?

The percent unit divides by 100 before the square root. Probability 0.0001 is the same as 0.01 percent.

Can this size a WAN circuit alone?

No. It is one loss-based bound.
