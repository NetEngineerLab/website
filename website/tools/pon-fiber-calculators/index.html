PON & Fiber Calculators

A PON or metro fiber design is a chain of decisions that only make sense together. Splitter ratio sets the loss floor, the cable route sets attenuation and delay, and the transceiver class decides whether the remaining budget is real. This hub collects the fiber and PON calculators already published on NetEngineerLab so you can walk that chain in one place. Start from the optical split, add fiber, splice and connector loss, then test the end-to-end power budget and the distance the class can still support. When the route is long enough for propagation to matter, estimate one-way delay and round-trip time, including route factor and equipment. If a field reading looks weak, compare it with the ONU receive-power check and the OTDR event list before you redesign the split. Optics choice, ODF and ODN resources, and dual OLT uplinks come last, once the optical numbers can become a build list. Each calculator keeps its own inputs and limits. Frequently asked questions

Which calculator should I open first?

Start with splitter loss when the PON ratio is still a choice, or with fiber loss when the route length and splice plan are the unknown. Power budget comes after both.

Does fiber latency change the optical power budget?

No. Latency and loss are different limits. A link can pass the power budget and still miss a delay target, so calculate both when the route is long.

When should I use OTDR instead of the loss calculator?

Use the loss calculator to predict a design. Use the OTDR event tool when you already have a trace and need to explain a splice, connector, bend or break.

Where do ODF and ODN resources fit?

After the optical budget and split are acceptable. The resource planner turns that design into ports, trays and a planning bill of materials.
