        How summarization works

        Route summarization folds several longer prefixes into fewer, shorter ones. The routing table and the routes you advertise get smaller. A fold depends on whether the addresses sit in the same parent network, not on whether the names look similar.

        1. Each prefix is normalized to its network address. 10.0.0.5/24 is written as 10.0.0.0/24.
        2. A prefix already covered by a shorter one is not advertised on its own. 10.0.0.0/25 is redundant once 10.0.0.0/24 is present.
        3. Remaining prefixes of the same length that are the pair inside one parent fold into that shorter prefix. 10.0.0.0/24 and 10.0.1.0/24 become 10.0.0.0/23.
        4. The step repeats until nothing else folds. If they become one route, that one route is the result. 10.0.1.0/24 and 10.0.2.0/24 are not a pair, so they stay as more than one route.
        5. IPv4 and IPv6 are summarized separately.

        A shorter prefix covers addresses that were not in the list. Confirm that space should not be advertised before you publish the summary.

        Limits

        • The summary follows address adjacency. It does not look at whether those routes exist in a network.
        • A shorter aggregate covers address space that was not in the list. Confirm that space should not be advertised before you publish the summary.
        • IPv4 and IPv6 stay separate.
        • The shortest prefix length stops further aggregation. It is not a router command.

        Questions

        Do two /24s always become a /23?

        Only when they are the pair inside one /23. 10.0.1.0/24 and 10.0.2.0/24 are not that pair, so they do not become 10.0.0.0/22.

        Can the result be pasted into a routing protocol?

        No. The result only compares address blocks. Advertising it, and keeping longer-prefix exceptions, still depends on the topology and the equipment notes.
