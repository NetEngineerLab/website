About and applicability

Use this for a DC feeder's ohmic drop. The default resistivity is 1/58 Ω·mm²/m, the IEC 60028 annealed-copper standard at 20 °C. It ignores contacts, bus joints and fault current.

This is an engineering approximation for planning. It is not a laboratory measurement and it does not guarantee equipment performance.

Content reviewed: 2026-11-19

How it is calculated

R = ρ_20 × [1 + α(T − 20)] × N × L / A; V_drop = I × R.

Resistance is resistivity times conductor paths times one-way length, divided by area. Two conductor paths cover the go and return. Resistivity at temperature T is ρ_20 × [1 + α(T−20)]. The default α, 0.00393 per °C, is an editable assumption. A check case is 1 A, 58 m, 1 mm², one path, 20 °C and ρ = 1/58: resistance is 1 Ω and drop is 1 V. PoE pair resistance is a different tool.

FAQ

Is 1/58 exact?

IEC 60028 states the standard resistance of copper as 1/58 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C. The page uses that fraction.

Does zero current fail?

No. The drop is zero.

Why can the end voltage be negative?

The arithmetic continues when the drop exceeds the source. Treat that as an unsuitable feeder, not as a measured polarity.

Is the alpha a cable rating?

No. Replace it with the coefficient you intend to use.
