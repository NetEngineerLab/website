Limits and broadening

Limits

Lengths that keep |D × L − C| within the tolerance T lie between (C·sign(D) − T) / |D| and (C·sign(D) + T) / |D|. Without compensation the lower bound is 0, so only the maximum is shown; an over-compensated link (for example D = 17, C = 2,000, T = 400) shows the full window 94.12–141.18 km. Pulse broadening is estimated as |residual CD| × Δλ in ps. The 80% warning threshold is this tool's screening rule, not a standard value.

Methodology and engineering notes

METHODOLOGY

How to use

  1. Keep the G.652 model or pick “Datasheet value” and enter D from the cable datasheet.
  2. Enter the wavelength, fiber length and any DCM or FBG compensation.
  3. Enter the receiver CD tolerance from the transceiver datasheet.
  4. Read residual CD, margin and the CD-limited length.

Dispersion formula

ITU-T G.652 defines the chromatic dispersion coefficient as D(λ) = (S0/4)·(λ − λ0⁴/λ³) ps/(nm·km), with λ0 between 1300 and 1324 nm and S0 ≤ 0.092 ps/(nm²·km). At 1550 nm with λ0 = 1310 nm and S0 = 0.092 this gives 17.461 ps/(nm·km). Accumulated CD = D × L; residual CD = D × L − compensation.

Worked examples

Worked examples

Worked case: 80 km at 1550 nm

G.652 model, λ0 1310 nm, S0 0.092: D = 17.461 ps/(nm·km). 80 km gives 1,396.8 ps/nm. Against a 1,600 ps/nm receiver the margin is 203.2 ps/nm (87.3% used, warning) and the CD-limited length is 91.63 km. With Δλ = 0.1 nm the broadening is 139.68 ps. Adding 680 ps/nm of compensation leaves 716.8 ps/nm residual.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

What is the chromatic dispersion of G.652 fiber at 1550 nm?

About 17 ps/(nm·km). The ITU-T G.652 model with λ0 = 1310 nm and S0 = 0.092 gives 17.46 ps/(nm·km); typical cables measure 16.5–18.

How do I calculate total chromatic dispersion?

Multiply the coefficient by the length: CD = D × L. 80 km × 17.46 ps/(nm·km) ≈ 1,397 ps/nm. Subtract any DCM or FBG compensation to get the residual.

What is a typical CD tolerance for 10G optics?

It depends on the module. Directly modulated 10G optics are often specified around 800 ps/nm, while 10G ER/ZR-class optics are often specified around 1,600 ps/nm. Always use the datasheet.

Does chromatic dispersion matter at 1310 nm?

Very little. 1310 nm is close to the zero-dispersion wavelength of G.652 fiber, so D is near 0 there.

Do coherent optics need dispersion compensation?

Usually not. Coherent receivers compensate CD electronically in the DSP and tolerate tens of thousands of ps/nm.

ENGINEERING REFERENCES

  • ITU-T G.652 (08/2024): single-mode fibre and cable – §6.10 equation (6-1) chromatic dispersion coefficient D(λ) = (S0/4)(λ − λ0⁴/λ³); λ0 1300–1324 nm and S0 ≤ 0.092 ps/(nm²·km) in clause 8 Table 1 (G.652.B) and Table 2 (G.652.D)
  • ITU-T G.650.1 (01/2024): test methods for linear attributes – §6.5 chromatic dispersion measurement, Annex A fitting
  • ITU-T G.691 (03/2006): optical interfaces for single-channel STM-64 and other SDH systems with optical amplifiers – §6.3.2.1 maximum chromatic dispersion per application code
  • ITU-T G.655 (11/2009): non-zero dispersion-shifted fibre – use the datasheet D for these fibres

Use the datasheet coefficient for G.655, G.654 or vendor fibers; the G.652 model applies to standard single-mode fiber. Last reviewed: 2026-10-20.
