Switching & Bandwidth Calculators

Switch and bandwidth planning fails when uplink math, address space and frame size are checked by different people with different spreadsheets. This hub lines up the switching, bandwidth, subnet and capacity calculators that already exist on NetEngineerLab. Begin with traffic you can defend: application rates or a transfer-time estimate, not a round number copied from a previous site. Turn that load into an access-port and uplink plan, including concurrency, utilization and what remains when one uplink fails. Only then cut the address plan. A subnet calculator defines the boundaries, and the VLAN capacity planner checks whether each VLAN still has hosts left after reservations. MTU belongs in the same pass, because a frame that does not fit the path looks like a capacity problem in production. Forecasting comes after the current design is explicit, so growth is applied to a known baseline. Fabric planning closes the sequence when the access switch is one tier of a larger network.

Frequently asked questions

Should oversubscription or bandwidth come first?

Estimate the offered load first, then test whether the proposed uplinks carry it with the concurrency and failure case you are willing to sign.

Is the VLAN planner a VLSM tool?

It plans usable host capacity per VLAN from a prefix and reservations. Use the subnet calculator first when you still need to draw the prefix boundaries.

Why is MTU in a bandwidth hub?

A frame that does not fit the path is dropped or fragmented. That shows up as a throughput failure even when the uplink arithmetic was correct.

When do I open the fabric capacity planner?

When the question moves from one access switch to how leaf, spine or aggregation tiers converge under the same traffic and failure assumptions.
