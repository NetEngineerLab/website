Data Center Power & Cooling Calculators

Power and cooling work is usually split across PoE, the rack, the room and the standby plant, which is how a design passes one spreadsheet and fails the next. This hub gathers the PoE, generator, PUE, rack, cooling, rectifier and battery calculators on NetEngineerLab. Start where the load is created. A PoE budget that ignores cable drop will look fine at the switch and fail at the device, so check source capacity and voltage at length together. Move those watts into the rack power and cooling view before you scale them to a room. PUE then shows how much extra electrical load the facility adds on top of the IT load. Cooling load and airflow containment test whether that heat can leave the row. Standby comes last and in layers: UPS runtime for the ride-through window, generator fuel for the outage you are designing to, and the transfer interval between them. Telecom batteries, rectifiers and breakers belong in that same conversation when the site is a communications room.

Frequently asked questions

Why are PoE and PUE in the same hub?

PoE is often part of the IT load that PUE multiplies. Leaving it out understates both the rack and the facility electrical load.

Do I size the generator from the UPS rating?

Not by itself. Check UPS runtime and the transfer ride-through first, then size generator fuel for the outage duration and the load that is actually connected.

Where does rack cooling stop and room cooling start?

Use the rack calculator for the cabinet. Use the cooling-load and airflow tools when the question is the room, the row and whether containment changes the airflow the units must deliver.

Which battery calculator should a telecom room use?

Use the −48 V runtime tool for the DC plant, and the UPS runtime tool for an AC UPS. Rectifier and breaker sizing sit beside the DC plant, not instead of it.
