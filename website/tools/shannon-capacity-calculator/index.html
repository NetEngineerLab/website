About and applicability

This is the AWGN capacity bound. A modem, a Wi-Fi MCS table, or an optical transponder can sit well below it. SNR may be entered in decibels or as a linear power ratio.

This is an engineering approximation for planning. It is not a laboratory measurement and it does not guarantee equipment performance.

Content reviewed: 2026-11-19

How it is calculated

C = B × log2(1 + S/N).

Shannon's AWGN formula is C = B log2(1 + S/N), where B is bandwidth in hertz and S/N is a linear power ratio. A decibel SNR of x becomes 10^(x/10). A worked case is B = 20 MHz and SNR = 20 dB, so S/N = 100 and C = 20000000 × log2(101). The result is an upper bound for additive white Gaussian noise with the bandwidth and SNR you enter. It is not a link budget and it does not include fading, interference structure, or protocol overhead.

FAQ

Is log2(101) part of the 20 dB example?

Yes. 20 dB is a linear ratio of 100, and the argument of the logarithm is 1+100.

Can SNR be 0 dB?

Yes. The linear ratio is 1 and the capacity is B × 1 bit/s/Hz.

Does a wider band always help?

Only in this formula, and only if the SNR you entered still applies to that wider band.

Is this a Wi-Fi throughput?

No. It is the AWGN bound.
