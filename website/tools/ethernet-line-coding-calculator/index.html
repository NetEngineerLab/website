About and applicability

Use this to turn a MAC payload rate into the higher line rate implied by a published Ethernet line code. It does not choose a PMD, a lane count, or a reach.

This is an engineering approximation for planning. It is not a laboratory measurement and it does not guarantee equipment performance.

Content reviewed: 2026-11-19

How it is calculated

Line rate = payload × (code bits / payload bits). RS-FEC multiplies an extra codeword ratio.

8b/10b expands 8 payload bits into 10 line bits, so the ratio is 10/8. 64b/66b uses 66/64. RS(528,514) adds 528/514 on top of 64b/66b. RS(544,514) adds 544/514 on top of 64b/66b. A 10 Gbps payload with 64b/66b is exactly 10.3125 Gbps. The calculator applies only that ratio. Auto-negotiation, alignment markers and a particular clause's lane mapping are outside the result.

FAQ

Is 10.3125 Gbps a measured rate?

It is the exact 66/64 expansion of 10 Gbps.

Does RS-FEC replace 64b/66b?

No. The presets multiply both ratios.

Can I paste this into a switch as a speed?

No. It is the line-rate arithmetic of the selected code.

Where do the ratios come from?

They are the published 8b/10b, 64b/66b, RS(528,514) and RS(544,514) sizes used by IEEE 802.3.
