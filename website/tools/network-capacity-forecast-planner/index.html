How to use the forecast

Use a measured baseline

Use busy-hour P95/P99 or another documented planning percentile from a representative period. Keep the capacity boundary and traffic direction consistent.

Choose a trigger and horizon

The threshold is an operational planning trigger. Set it from latency, loss, queueing, service commitments and procurement lead time rather than assuming a universal safe percentage.

Read the expansion date

Months to threshold assumes a constant compound growth rate. Treat it as a planning estimate and update it as measured growth changes.

Validate the capacity plan

Review surviving capacity under failures, path diversity, traffic asymmetry, hardware limits, queue drops and link procurement lead time before approving an upgrade.

Engineering boundaries

This browser-only planner does not infer traffic from raw counters, model seasonality, queue behavior, ECMP imbalance or failure topology. Its growth projection is a screening model; validate with telemetry and a design review.

Frequently asked questions

How should I choose the annual growth rate?

Fit a trend to several months or quarters of comparable busy-hour measurements and document known changes such as new sites, users or applications.

Does the expansion month guarantee there will be no congestion?

No. It is based on the supplied average baseline and growth assumption. Short peaks, imbalance or failures can exceed the threshold earlier.

Why can required capacity exceed installed capacity well before the horizon?

The plan reserves the specified target utilization as headroom. Required capacity is forecast peak demand divided by that target.

Method: compound growth and target-utilization sizing. Review against live telemetry before procurement.
