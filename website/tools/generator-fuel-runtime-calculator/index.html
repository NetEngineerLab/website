Engineering notes

Fuel(period) = interpolated manufacturer L/h × operating hours

Preliminary planning only. Verify exact manufacturer fuel data, altitude/temperature derating, transient load, minimum-load limits, tank usable volume, maintenance and local fuel-storage requirements before implementation.

Per-period fuel calculation
Fuel = fuel rate (L/h) × runtime (h)
Outage fuel = runtime fuel + reserve + refill allowance
Interpolate manufacturer curve between nearest load points

Load-profile fuel

For each period, interpolate fuel rate from the manufacturer curve at that load, then multiply by hours. Do not substitute a no-load rate for a loaded rate.

Tank runtime

Usable fuel is tank volume minus unusable volume and reserve. Runtime is usable fuel divided by average fuel rate under the defined profile.

Reserve and logistics

Target outage fuel includes engineering reserve and refill logistics. Battery assist can delay generator start and reduce fuel burned.

Generator fuel and runtime method

Fuel used is fuel rate (L/h) × runtime (h). For a load curve, interpolate between the two nearest manufacturer points; do not substitute a no-load rate for a loaded rate. Required outage fuel is runtime fuel plus the specified reserve and refill logistics allowance.

Example: a curve gives 8.0 L/h at 50% load and 11.5 L/h at 75%. At 60% load, linear interpolation estimates 9.4 L/h; a 12-hour outage needs about 112.8 L before reserve. Validate the estimate against the exact engine, altitude, ambient temperature and fuel quality.

Limits and related planning

This calculator cannot model transient step loading, wet stacking, derating or transfer delays without manufacturer data. Combine it with the generator fuel planning guide and UPS runtime calculator for ride-through.

FAQ

Why use a manufacturer curve? Diesel consumption changes materially with load and engine model; a generic L/kWh value can misstate outage reserve.

Does runtime equal tank volume divided by rate? Only as a first estimate; reserve, pickup limits, refill access and changing load reduce usable runtime.

Engineering FAQ

Why use a manufacturer fuel curve instead of a generic fuel-rate formula?

Diesel consumption varies by engine, alternator, emissions package and operating point. Enter the exact 25/50/75/100% fuel table when available; the built-in values are an editable example only.

How is fuel consumption between load points calculated?

The tool uses linear interpolation between the adjacent manufacturer load points, then multiplies the resulting litres per hour by the duration of each load period.

What does fuel reserve mean?

The reserve percentage is fuel intentionally kept unavailable for the planned outage. You can also enter explicitly unusable tank volume for pickup, tank geometry or operating policy.

How does battery assistance affect the result?

Battery assistance is treated as an energy-equivalent delay before generator operation, based on usable battery kWh, discharge efficiency and average load. It is a planning estimate, not a battery discharge-curve simulation.

Can this replace generator manufacturer sizing and fuel-system design?

No. Final design must verify the exact model fuel table, altitude and temperature derating, minimum loading, transient starting load, tank usable volume, fuel return system, maintenance limits and local storage requirements.
