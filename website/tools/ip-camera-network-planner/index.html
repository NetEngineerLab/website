Design inputs

Bitrate is an engineering estimate from resolution, codec and frame rate. Calibrate it with the camera peak bitrate and the scene. The loaded values are an engineering example (100 cameras, H.265, 15 FPS, 15 W, 30 days), not a vendor datasheet.

Core calculation

per-camera bitrate = resolution tier × codec factor × (FPS / 15) × 8; total bandwidth = bitrate × camera count × 1.20; PoE budget = camera count × watts × 1.25; raw storage TB = total Mbps × days × 0.0108

How the estimate is built

Base bitrate comes from the resolution and codec tier, then scales linearly with frame rate. Total bandwidth adds 20% for protocol and burst headroom. Raw NVR capacity uses Mbps × days × 0.0108 to get decimal terabytes (TB). Recommended usable capacity keeps another 15%. The uplink is twice the total bandwidth, rounded up to 1/10/25/40/100G.

The N-1 check treats one access switch or one uplink as failed. Use dual uplinks, a stack or MLAG, spread cameras over at least two independent paths, and make either remaining path able to carry the full video load. A single uplink is reported as not meeting N-1.
