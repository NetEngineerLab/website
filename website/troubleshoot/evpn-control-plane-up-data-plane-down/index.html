EVPN Control Plane Up, Data Plane Down Lab

Diagnose size-dependent EVPN/VXLAN loss through eight interactive stages and ten evidence checkpoints.

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

1. Symptom

CE-A198.51.100.10/24 and CE-B198.51.100.20/24 share VLAN100/VNI10100/BD100 across two leaves.1472-byte ICMPv4 echo payload with DF fails in both directions;32-byte probes pass. This is size-dependent loss, not every ping failing.

CE-A ↔ LeafA/VTEP192.0.2.11 ↔ Underlay ↔ LeafB/VTEP192.0.2.12 ↔ CE-B

2. Known Good Evidence

BGP EVPN is Established; expected MAC routes are imported and installed; VNI10100 is up. These facts establish signaling context. They do not test the complete encapsulated packet envelope.

3. Ask: next check?

Select a check, reveal its observation and explain what it can and cannot establish. Inspect all ten distinct checkpoints before narrowing the fault domain.

5. Narrow fault domain

Use the complete evidence set to choose a fault domain. Healthy small packets and service mapping must be reconciled with size-correlated drops.

6. Final diagnosis

Choose the cause that accounts for1422/1423-byte boundary behavior and the observed egress MTU drops.

7. Verification

Educational simulation only. First validate every relevant link/path and both directions plus the platform IP-MTU convention. The post-fix1600 profile is one fixture, not a universal recommendation. Reducing service MTU is an alternative with endpoint/application trade-offs.

8. Lessons learned

Completion requires the supported diagnosis and a simulated verification run. Healthy control-plane state does not prove delivery; evidence must connect endpoint resolution, service mapping, actual encapsulation, packet-size boundaries and correlated drops.

  1. Keep packet-size/DF/direction context with every probe.
  2. Treat IPv6 ND and anycast as conditional checks, not universal suspects.
  3. Revalidate the full path and recovery after a controlled change.
